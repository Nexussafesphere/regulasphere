Nigeria ICT Innovation and Entrepreneurship Vision
Foreword
Technological advancement is accelerating and the global economy is transforming in
response. The majority of Nigerians are at serious risk of being left behind unless there is an
urgent technological revolution that can transform agriculture, education, and all other spheres
of public life. The Nigerian technology entrepreneurship ecosystem is gaining increasing
international attention and attracting Foreign Direct Investment. To further unlock our national
potential in technology innovation and entrepreneurship, National Information Technology
Development Agency (NITDA) created its Special Purpose Vehicle, Office for ICT Innovation
and Entrepreneurship (OIIE) to build a sustainable and thriving technology startup ecosystem
in Nigeria. OIIE drives this through policies, initiatives and programs that will support innovation
and technology entrepreneurship.
The agency has been engaging with stakeholders (such as investors, hubs, entrepreneurs,
innovators, academia, government) to collectively identify challenges encountered in the
ecosystem and determine ways to address the challenges. A key challenge identified by
entrepreneurs is lack of clarity and access to existing government incentives. Others includes
limited access to business support, limited access to market and capital for entrepreneurs. It
became apparent that there needs to be specific policies and incentives for the different sectors
of the ecosystem to encourage entrepreneurship, increase access to digital infrastructure,
increase research and development, bridging the digital gender divide, increase affordable
internet access for all and soon.
Indeed, Technology has the ability to unite all and remove barriers to economic transformation.
Thus, this document is a national unifying policy vision that proposes a number of measures to
connect, educate and enable digital innovation in Nigeria. We believe that if implemented in a
collaborative and engaging approach, it will result in an increase in employment, innovation in
the delivery of government services and an increase in the contribution of ICT to GDP. The
ministry of Communication through NITDA will lead to the implementation of the policy vision.

Nigeria ICT Innovation and Entrepreneurship Vision
Introduction
The National Information Technology Development Agency (NITDA) was established in 2001
to regulate the IT sector in general. Section 6 (i & l) of the NITDA act empowers it to make policy
recommendations to develop technology in Nigeria. To foster the growth of the technology
ecosystem, NITDA created its subsidiary, Office for ICT Innovation and Entrepreneurship (OIIE)
to drive the innovation and entrepreneurship mandate of the agency.
The mandates for the establishment of OIIE include: develop, establish, support and incentivise
ICT innovation hubs, information technology parks and community enterprise hubs across
Nigeria; and, collaborate with the private sector, development partners and MDAs at both
federal and state levels on policies and programs that support ICT entrepreneurship and
commercialisation.
NITDA through OIIE has been listening to stakeholders and as a result, there is a need to
reposition as agile facilitators to create an enabling environment for technology
entrepreneurship to thrive. In this regard, NITDA plans to influence government policies by
sharing findings from stakeholder engagements and research findings from similar markets to
Nigeria.
This document, the Nigeria ICT Innovation and Entrepreneurship Vision (NIIEV), comprises of
policy recommendations and incentives designed to strengthen the Nigerian technology
entrepreneurship ecosystem. It consists of the following sections: Digital Infrastructure;
Education Reform, Skills Development and R&D; and, Support the Ecosystem for Innovation
and Entrepreneurship. The document is also accompanied with an information note that
provides additional background information on each vision statement.

DIGITAL INFRASTRUCTURE
Nigeria will significantly increase access to ICT and provide universal and affordable
access to the Internet. By 2025, 95% of the population will have access to broadband
Internet.
1. Incentivise telecommunication infrastructure sharing to achieve the economies of
scale necessary for universal affordable access. Government will support private sector
providers to merge their infrastructure.

4. Build a national data framework and harmonise e-Governance to enable innovation,
improve efficiency, transparency, accountability and citizen participation in government;
a. Build a single digital platform that aligns access to government datasets and to
electronically process Freedom of Information (FOI) requests for every MDA;
b. Amend the FOI Act to strengthen enforcement and compliance; and
c. Harmonise e-Governance by using a Whole of Government (WoG) approach to
rationalise and simplify access to government services.
5. Support renewable energy through easily accessible tax incentives for local production
of renewable energy technology.

EDUCATION REFORM, SKILLS DEVELOPMENT AND R&D

MOU7 to protect the infrastructure. This will go a long way in alleviating this challenge which will
in turn increase access to affordable broadband. Furthermore, the government plans to lay

Vision 2: Drop Right of Way charges for fibre distribution and simplify administrative
requirements to reduce capital requirements for expanding digital infrastructure, thereby
reducing the costs passed on to consumers.
Agencies: Federal Ministry of Communication Technology (FMCT), Federal Inland Revenue
Services (FIRS), Federal Ministry of Power, Works and Housing, State Inland Revenue Board,
Local Government Authority, Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC).
Rationale: Lack of harmonised Right of Way (RoW) across Federal, State and LGA and multiple
taxation increases the cost of service delivery which is passed on to the consumers. Elimination
of such charges will encourage investments and reduce the cost of service delivery.
Status: Harmonisation and reduction of RoW charges is already under review.
Currently, there is a guideline for the grant and access RoW to ICT services on Federal roads
by the Federal Ministry of Power, Works and Housing11. The charges are N145 per linear meter
for ducts and cables and N20 per linear metre annual maintenance fees subject to change every
5 years. States and Local Governments have different charges and this varies from state to
state. The lack of uniform charges is one of the challenges telcos face which affects the supply
of services. To address this challenge, the National Economic Council (NEC) approved a policy
on harmonisation of right of way charges across LG, States and Federal highways12. The NEC
RoW guideline still maintains the amount of N145 per meter for laying fibre for all parts of the
country13. Also, NCC made a case to the Governors Forum to ensure on the need to adhere to
the NEC’s resolutions14. RoW is included in the NCC’s Guidelines on Collocation and
Infrastructure Sharing15, this provides a means of bringing down the cost of broadband as shown
in Vision 116.
The Industrial Council is working with NEC on addressing Multiple Taxation. NEC approved the
adoption of the 2015 Approved List of Taxes by all states.

Vision 9: Simplify immigration requirements for investors, foreign companies and other
skilled professionals to start businesses (or invest) in Nigeria to facilitate exchange programs
and collaborations.
Agencies: Nigerian Immigration Service (NIS), Federal Ministry of Interior (FMI), Ministry of
Foreign Affairs (MoFA), Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC).
Rationale: There is considerable merit in reducing the current visa system in Nigeria as such,
the targeted investors/ tourist will be interested in coming down to the country which will facilitate
knowledge transfer by getting the best, also it will have great impact on our GDP.
Status: Executive Order 001 mandates the processing of tourist and business visas within 48
hours and the publishing of requirements for visa on arrival on websites of Nigerian embassies,
high commissions and other immigration related agencies40. In the same Executive Order,
MDAs are mandated to publish the list and requirements with timeline, fees and mode of
communication for these services.
